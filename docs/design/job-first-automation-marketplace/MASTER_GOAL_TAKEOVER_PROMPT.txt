1. THE OWNER'S REQUEST AND THE SERVICE TO BUILD

The owner explicitly wants to test all of this:

1) Receive the video. Save the original footage, permissions and job identity.
   The customer records or uploads. No written description is required.
2) Understand the job. Existing analysis drafts the operation, objects,
   starting positions, destination, variations and likely constraints. The
   customer corrects that reading and supplies important facts the video
   cannot establish.
3) Prepare the site simulation. Reconstruct the workspace, prepare the objects
   and connect the task to a measurable outcome.
4) Shortlist complete configurations. Find plausible combinations of robot,
   gripper, cameras, controller and policy that Blueprint can actually run
   and someone can deliver.
5) Run matched evaluations. Give shortlisted configurations the same relevant
   job conditions. Measure successful work, speed, failures and human help.
   Keep the evidence behind every result.
6) Prepare a recommendation and delivery proposals. Combine technical results
   with equipment availability, installation cost, operating cost and support.

The owner then said: "i want to make this durable and work for all sites/tasks."
They know Atlas is not available yet. Do not make Atlas a prerequisite or list
it as runnable because a robot model or policy announcement exists.

The broader goal is to become the default place to submit a robot job and get
the best deliverable option. Real jobs, independent comparison, usable supplier
access, reliable delivery partners and observed outcomes build that position.
This request does not ask for a social network, public bidding marketplace,
payments system, a universal robot foundation model, or a new website redesign.

After stage 6, preserve the existing next steps: the customer chooses a real
proposal; customer and deployment partner agree a bounded physical pilot;
Blueprint records the actual outcome and, where appropriate, joins genuine
physical evidence to the earlier prediction. Selection is not contract award.

5. SIX-STAGE IMPLEMENTATION CONTRACT

Stage 1: capture and job identity

Use existing recorder/upload, consent and storage mechanisms. Retain original
bytes in authorized company storage, media digest, provenance, permission scope,
job/site/capture identity and receipt. Successful storage/receipt is the source
of truth, not a checkbox saying footage exists or an ID allocated before upload.
Draft creation may precede upload. Assessment may not.

Preserve upload recovery, anonymous/private links and account handoff. A retry
or reload must find the existing job/upload rather than silently create another.
Record permission is not consent for training, public case studies or sending
unbounded footage to arbitrary vendors. Use existing permission scopes.

Stage 2: understand and confirm the job

Reconnect the existing video reader and brief-merging path. Derive the summary
and structured facts from the same analysis output; do not pay for a second
extraction to populate the description. Preserve customer-authored/confirmed
text and source-bound revision handling. Ask only for material unknowns, such as
weights or dimensions that the current footage cannot establish.

Represent observed, measured, customer-declared and inferred values distinctly.
Do not turn an estimated dimension into an authoritative hardware exclusion.
Identify operation/task family, relevant objects and properties, start/goal,
variants, frequency, required quality/throughput and human interaction where
available. Missing facts remain unknown; do not fabricate a complete task.

Stage 3: capture-backed simulation and measurable task

Reuse capture -> reconstruction -> site package -> task testbed. Bind retained
video/observed frames, metric geometry, object assets, collision/dynamics, sensor
setup and task success definition through the existing contracts. Retain exact
source and revision identities. Reuse valid artifacts instead of reconstructing
on every refresh. A stale artifact must not be relabeled current.

"Accurate" is task-specific. A visually attractive reconstruction alone cannot
establish scale, mass, friction, articulation, contact or sensor equivalence.
Use the existing measurement/fidelity routing for quantities that could change
the decision. Request the missing observation or physical measurement in the
existing flow; do not build a universal questionnaire or call a splat physics.

Task-specific object preparation, dynamics, graders and reset logic belong in
the relevant adapters. Multi-step or articulated work must declare its required
capabilities; it must not be silently coerced into a pick/place fixture.
Preserve metric units, coordinate frames and calibration across these artifacts;
an asset transform that renders correctly can still be wrong for robot control.

Stage 4: complete configurations and supportable shortlist

Reuse the actual capability/policy/embodiment registries. A reproducible candidate
includes robot/configuration, gripper/tool, camera/sensor placement, observation
and action schemas, units/normalization, controller/rate, policy identity/version,
adapter/runtime and deployment assumptions. For hosted APIs also retain the
available provider deployment identity, latency and availability assumptions.

Screen physical plausibility, actual executable and licensed access, task
compatibility, supportable delivery and economics before expensive evaluation.
Do not run every policy on every robot. A researched vendor is not an integrated
endpoint, a robot adapter is not universal policy compatibility, and a supplier
name is not a delivery commitment.

Maintain distinct executable-technology access, deployment capability, and a
specific partner's scoped proposal. Reuse supplier onboarding across jobs.
Research-assisted ranking may suggest whom to investigate; it cannot invent
tested compatibility, inventory, quotes or willingness to support a site.

Stage 5: actual matched policy execution

Use existing managed evaluation launch/allocator/runtime/result paths, with
source-bound immutable configuration and execution IDs. Step the environment
with the actual policy responses and score independently. A plan, provider
"success" response, job queued message, rendered clip or read-only evidence
adapter is not a policy evaluation.

Use matched task distributions, applicable seeds, resets and held-out conditions.
Preserve intended hardware differences in full-system comparisons. The same-body
policy experiment and comparing different complete systems answer different
questions; report their scope rather than inventing a universal leaderboard.

Measure success/quality, failures, interventions, throughput/cycle time where
supported, and uncertainties. Distinguish simulated time from real end-to-end
cycle time including inference, network, resets and assistance. Retain policy
inputs/outputs, episode lifecycle, artifacts, failures and scorer provenance.
Unmeasured assistance, costs or cycle time remain unmeasured, never zero.
Do not expose evaluator ground truth or private assets to a policy endpoint.

Run only with existing execution authority and bounded cost/resource controls.
Reuse cancellation, teardown and spend accounting. New broad autonomy or clearing
a founder stop is not implied by wiring the flow together.

Stage 6: evidence, recommendation and delivery

Publish actual technical results to the correct current job/task/scene with
signed receipts. Project plain-language findings in the existing workspace:
which options ran, what happened, which limits remain and the recommended next
action. Distinguish not tested, failed, cancelled and unsupported.

Recommend only within technical evidence and delivery support. Permit no suitable
option, missing-input and abstention results without a fabricated winner. The
scientific result is separate from the commercial choice. A lower quote cannot
change a policy's measured outcome.

Create comparable proposal drafts using the same job scope and acceptance terms.
Obtain real availability/cost/support commitments from actual partners before
presenting a proposal as theirs. Unknown costs stay unknown. Model-inference
cost is one part of operation, not the full robot economics. Do not invent a
provider pricing formula or hardware utilization assumption.

Contact timing: site for video/permissions and material unknowns; deployers during
shortlisting for support/availability and after scope/evidence for quotes; policy
vendors only where access, terms or interface integration is missing. Existing
API access should remove repeated per-job invitations where possible. Prepare
messages/scopes now; do not send external outreach without applicable explicit
authorization. No automatic awards, negotiation bot or bidding war is needed.

6. WHAT "DURABLE FOR ALL SITES/TASKS" MEANS IN CODE

Implement this through the existing stores, queues, contracts and workers. Do
not introduce a generic workflow platform, new cloud service or parallel state
database merely to satisfy the word durable.

- Site/job/task/capture IDs are data, not hard-coded demo constants. Multiple
  concurrent jobs and tenants must stay isolated. Owner links and staff authority
  remain distinct; unauthenticated users cannot read another site's evidence.
- Derive stage completion from actual retained receipts/artifacts. Persist
  progress before/after external effects using the existing intent/outbox/lease
  patterns. Process memory alone cannot own a customer's job.
- Assume queues/webhooks are at least once. Duplicate upload notifications,
  signed result retries and worker restarts must not duplicate reconstruction,
  paid evaluation, proposals or external sends. Use existing idempotency keys,
  immutable identities, durable claims and reconciliation receipts.
- Resume from the last valid artifact after a crash. Distinguish retryable
  transient failures from a permanent incompatibility or a specific missing
  input. Bounded retries/backoff/timeouts, dead-letter/retry surfaces and leases
  should reuse existing mechanisms. Do not retry forever or lose the reason.
- An ambiguous provider timeout is not permission to purchase another run. First
  reconcile whether the original request exists and incurred spend.
- Bind results to technical scope. Quote/budget edits should not invalidate
  unchanged scientific observations. Changed video/task/configuration/testbed
  must affect technical applicability; late old results stay historical and
  cannot overwrite the new job revision's recommendation.
- Cancellation, deletion/withdrawal and permission changes must stop applicable
  future work and preserve the minimum existing audit/provenance obligations.
  Do not keep calling a provider using revoked permission.
- Artifact links, schemas and identity must be company-controlled and portable.
  Use existing authorized object storage and database, not this chat/session or
  one model vendor's ephemeral file IDs as canonical storage. Keep bounded
  summaries/references in Firestore, large immutable artifacts in object storage.
- Explain an interruption using the existing progress/error surface and expose
  the existing retry/correction action where appropriate. No operations dashboard
  redesign is needed.

For task/robot extensibility, inspect current abstractions before adding types:

- Separate task requirements (work and scoring) from site geometry/objects,
  embodiment/tool/sensors, policy interface and delivery availability.
- Turn current rigid-object, fixed-arm and two-policy assumptions into explicit
  first supported profiles/adapters where they currently act as global rules.
  Preserve the current proof protocol as its own bounded experiment.
- Route by declared capabilities and validated compatibility, with measured
  parameters and explicit unavailable reasons. Avoid growing provider-name or
  site-name conditionals throughout the core workflow.
- Existing task catalogs include rigid_pick_place and articulated_open_close
  fidelity templates. Templates are not proof of an executable articulated-task
  adapter or reviewed request-specific thresholds. Reuse real support and state
  missing runtime/scorer assets precisely.
- New sites using a supported profile should require data/capture/parameters,
  not source-code edits. A new task family or embodiment may require assets,
  adapters, reset/scoring and evidence work. Make the extension seam clear and
  verify it; do not pretend all future integrations require no engineering.
- Arbitrary task submissions must be accepted into truthful routing. Unsupported
  work should retain the video/job and explain the capability gap. It must not
  crash, fabricate a run, disappear, or be declared technically impossible solely
  because the current catalog lacks an adapter.
- Atlas and other unavailable robots/providers remain unavailable entries or
  future capabilities, without blocking real supported configurations. Do not
  add mock success to a production path as a substitute for access.
