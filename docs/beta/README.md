# Blueprint beta

This folder explains the invited beta as it works today. The frame is the
shared block of [`PLATFORM_CONTEXT.md`](../../PLATFORM_CONTEXT.md) (owner
direction 2026-11-12).

## How the beta works

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1. Email and video | A site sends an email address and a video of one recurring job. |
| 2. Job packet | Blueprint turns the video into a job packet: the site's sentence for the job, a first look on paper, a simulation of the site, and what counts as done. Where one exists, the result of our early trial of openly available robot software. |
| 3. Deployment companies | Sharing is the service: Blueprint shares the job with the deployment companies it has approved, and records their responses. Today the website still shares a summary only, after the site's share step; the change is in build. |
| 4. Where the job stands | The site's job page says plainly: not yet sent, sent and waiting, or the responses given. |

- The site pays nothing. Deployment companies will pay; how is not decided,
  and no price is stated anywhere. Each introduction is recorded as a billable event. Nothing is charged and no price is stated until the owner sets one.
- No person at Blueprint stands between a site's video and an introduction. When a job is ready it is shown to every approved deployment company, and those that asked for job emails are told. People act only where their own decision is the point: a site sends, confirms, asks for an introduction and can withdraw; a company applies and answers; Blueprint approves a company once. The site asks for an introduction with one press, at most three per job; Blueprint then gives each side the other's name and contact details by email. Nothing else shares them. After an introduction both sides are asked, at 14, 45 and 90 days, what happened; the answer is recorded on the job. The
  website change for this is in build.
- Sharing is the service. A site that sends a job agrees once, in the statement
  it accepts (recording statement `2026-11-12.v4` or later), that Blueprint
  shares the job with the deployment companies it has approved: the site's
  description, its video without its sound, the simulation built from it, the
  success rule, the first look and any early trial. There is no share button,
  no share setting and no pause. A job under an older statement is not shown
  to any company until its site accepts the current one. A site's name, street
  address and contact details are never shared without asking the site first.
  The website change for this is in build.
- Nothing promises a response that has not been given.
- The only hard stops are privacy, rights and money. Anything else that is
  missing is flagged, and the job continues.
- A simulation never certifies physical performance or safety.
