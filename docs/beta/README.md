# Blueprint beta

This folder explains the invited beta as it works today. The frame is the
shared block of [`PLATFORM_CONTEXT.md`](../../PLATFORM_CONTEXT.md) (owner
direction 2026-11-12).

## How the beta works

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1. Email and video | A site sends an email address and a video of one recurring job. |
| 2. Job packet | Blueprint turns the video into a job packet: the site's sentence for the job, a first look on paper, and a simulation of the site. Blueprint runs no robot software in the simulation (the early trial was dropped on 2026-11-14). |
| 3. Deployment companies | Sharing is the service: Blueprint shares the job with the deployment companies it has approved, and records their responses. |
| 4. Where the job stands | The site's job page says plainly where the job stands and shows each response. |

- The site pays nothing. Deployment companies will pay; how is not decided,
  and no price is stated anywhere. Each introduction is recorded as a billable
  event. Nothing is charged and no price is stated until the owner sets one.
- No person at Blueprint stands between a site's video and an introduction.
  When a job is ready it is shown to every approved deployment company, and
  each is emailed unless it turned job emails off. People act only where their
  own decision is the point: a site sends, confirms, accepts the companies it
  wants to meet and can withdraw; a company applies and answers; Blueprint
  approves a company once, by a published rule checked automatically or with
  one press. When the site presses Accept beside a company, Blueprint emails
  the company the site's email address and the place the site gave, and emails
  the site the company's name and contact details (the statement beside
  Accept says this). Nothing else shares them. After an introduction both sides are asked, at 14, 45 and 90 days,
  what happened; the answer is recorded on the job.
- Sharing is the service. A site that sends a job agrees once, in the
  statement it accepts (recording statement `2026-11-23.v6` today), that
  Blueprint shares the job with the deployment companies it has approved: the
  site's description, its video without its sound, the simulation built from
  it, the success rule, and the city and state. The first look stays with the
  site. There is no share button, no share setting and no pause. A job under
  an older statement is not shown to any company until its site accepts the
  current one. A site's name, street address and contact details are never
  shared without asking the site first.
- A company sees jobs only once it is approved and has accepted the Terms.
  Until one has, no company can see any job; the site's job page says when one
  can.
- Many simulations built so far have been the room only, with nothing in it
  set up as a separate object a robot can move. The site's job page and the
  company's page say so for each job.
- Nothing promises a response that has not been given.
- The only hard stops are privacy, rights and money. Anything else that is
  missing is flagged, and the job continues.
- A simulation never certifies physical performance or safety.
