where this file and that block differ, the block is right.*

## Product

**One-liner:** A site films a job once. Blueprint turns it into a job packet
that any robot deployment company can evaluate without visiting, sends it to
deployment companies it has approved, and tells the site who will take the job
and on what evidence.

**Our edge:** the site side: real jobs arriving as videos. The simulation of the
site is how a job becomes testable without a visit; it is a means, not the
product.

**The job packet:** the site's own sentence for the job and its constraints; the
video with the site's rights recorded; the first look on paper (which kinds of
robot most likely fit, from published specifications and a cited track
record); the simulation of the site with the job's object in it; the success
rule; and, where one exists, the result of our early trial of openly available
robot software. The early trial is not the site's answer and does not predict a
deployment company's robot.

**Sharing is the service.** A site that sends a job agrees once, in the
statement it accepts (recording statement `2026-11-12.v4` or later), that
Blueprint shares the job with the deployment companies it has approved: the
site's description, its video without its sound, the simulation built from it,
the success rule, the first look and any early trial. There is no share button,
no share setting and no pause. A site's name, street address and contact
details are never shared without asking the site first. The website and its
statements do not say this yet; that change is in build, and public copy
follows it, not this file.

## Who it is for

| Party | Role | What they get | Pays? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Site | The user | One video in; a first look, a simulation of their site, and the responses of deployment companies. | No. Free. |
| Deployment company | The customer | Qualified jobs they would not otherwise see, in one format. | Will pay; how is not decided. Each introduction is recorded as a billable event. No price is stated anywhere. |

## How it runs

No person at Blueprint stands between a site's video and an introduction. When a job is ready it is shown to every approved deployment company, and those that asked for job emails are told. People act only where their own decision is the point: a site sends, confirms, asks for an introduction and can withdraw; a company applies and answers; Blueprint approves a company once. The site asks for an introduction with one press for each company it wants to meet; Blueprint then gives each side the other's name and contact details by email. Nothing else shares them. After an introduction both sides are asked, at 14, 45 and 90 days, what happened; the answer is recorded on the job. The website does not
do this yet; the change is in build. No deployment company is named in public copy.

## Rules for copy

- Don't make things up. Every sentence is true on the day it ships.
- No price, no promise of a response, no named customer or company.
- Until the website change ships, never say or imply that a company sees a
  site's video or simulation: no site has accepted `2026-11-12.v4` yet.
- Do not call a packet or a trial "proof"; a simulation never certifies
  physical performance or safety.

## Words

"Deployment company" (not vendor, integrator or OEM), "job packet", "first
look", "simulation of your site", "early trial", "responses". Say **job** to
customers.
